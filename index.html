The Natural Half-Step Rule

In Western music, the musical alphabet consists of 7 natural notes: A - B - C - D - E - F - G.

• B & C are separated by 1 Fret (Half-Step)

• E & F are separated by 1 Fret (Half-Step)

• All other adjacent natural notes are 2 Frets (Whole-Step) apart.

Between whole steps lie the accidental notes (Sharps ♯ / Flats ♭).

2

The 12th-Fret Octave Mirror

The 12th fret marks the exact mathematical midpoint of the guitar string length.

• Fret 0 (Open String) = Fret 12 (Octave)

• Fret 1 notes are mirrored at Fret 13

• Frets 13–24 are identical in layout to Frets 1–12, exactly one octave higher!

Once you memorize frets 1 to 12, you automatically know 13 to 24.

Utility & Application Tutorial

Transform theoretical knowledge into instinct. Learn how to navigate strings instantly using anchors, shapes, and targeted routines.

1. The 5th-Fret Anchor

Most adjacent guitar strings are tuned 5 semitones (a perfect 4th) apart. Playing the 5th fret on a lower string gives you the exact pitch of the open string above it!

  • • E string 5th Fret = Open A
  • • A string 5th Fret = Open D
  • • D string 5th Fret = Open G
  • • G string 4th Fret = Open B *
  • • B string 5th Fret = Open E

* Note the B-string shift at the 4th fret due to major 3rd tuning offset.

2. Octave Shapes

Instead of counting frets, use geometric octave patterns to jump across the fretboard instantly without losing your position.

Lower Strings (6 & 5):

Down 2 Strings + Right 2 Frets

Upper Strings (4 & 3):

Down 2 Strings + Right 3 Frets

This framework locks into the classic CAGED system seamlessly.

3. Daily Practice Drill

A 5-minute daily drill to build subconscious fretboard recall:

  1. Pick a single note randomly (e.g. F#).
  2. Find and play F# on string 6, then string 5, down to string 1.
  3. Repeat backwards from string 1 to 6.
  4. Use a metronome at 60 BPM (1 note per beat).

Consistency over speed: 5 minutes daily builds permanent muscle memory.
