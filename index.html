Introduction: Unlocking the Neck

For most guitarists, the fretboard looks like a complex maze of random dots and metal bars. However, underneath the surface lies a clean, predictable mathematical matrix. Once you learn how the musical alphabet maps onto the strings, the entire layout unfolds logically.

Core Philosophy: Stop memorizing random notes in isolation. Instead, understand the intervals and the relationship between strings and frets.

The Natural Notes & The Two Special Rules

Western music relies on 7 natural musical alphabet notes: A – B – C – D – E – F – G. On a piano, these correspond to the white keys. On a guitar, moving up by one fret means moving up by one half-step (semitone).

Between most of these natural notes sit "in-between" notes (sharps ♯ / flats ♭). But there is a crucial catch every guitarist must memorize:

  • There is no sharp/flat between B and C. They are right next to each other (1 fret apart).
  • There is no sharp/flat between E and F. They are also right next to each other (1 fret apart).

Every other natural note pair has a fret in between them (e.g., A to B has A♯/B♭).
